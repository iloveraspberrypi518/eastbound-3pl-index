    When to book

    When a deadline is coming (a tariff increase, Lunar New Year factory closures, holiday stock), every importer on your lane tries to sail before it, and rates climb as the last sailings fill up. Sailing early costs money too: the goods sit in a warehouse. This model finds where the crowd's bookings settle once nobody can save by switching weeks (an equilibrium), then picks your cheapest sailing given your own cargo.

    Every sailing in the window

    Assumptions: an empty week costs today's 40' rate for your origin, adjusted for the current scenario (edit it in the Mode advisor). Rush bookings are counted in weeks of spare capacity on the lane, and a week that's exactly full costs double. Holding cost is 25% a year of cargo value (the usual inventory carrying cost) plus $75 a week of storage. The rest of the crowd is a typical importer with $80,000 of cargo per container. Booking is assumed 2 weeks before sailing. Transit time comes from the route planner's fastest ocean route. Bookings that need goods ready earlier than your factory can deliver aren't ruled out, so check production dates.

    Tariffs & duties

    Estimate US import duties and the landed cost of a shipment from China, Vietnam or India. It stacks the base (MFN) duty, Section 301 duties, Section 232 metals duties and customs fees, then adds freight at today's published rates. The HTS codes are examples. Confirm the classification and rates with a licensed customs broker before you rely on them.

    What's in force (Oct 2026)

      Customs value is the goods value. International freight isn't dutiable in the US. The MPF is 0.3464% (FY2027 minimum $34.58, maximum $670.86). The HMF is 0.125% on ocean cargo only. Section 232 goods are exempt from the forced-labor 301 duty. Antidumping and countervailing duties aren't calculated here.

      Emissions

      Which route puts the least carbon in the air? Each option is scored in kg of CO₂-equivalent per tonne of cargo, door to door. The score is distance on each leg × a standard emission factor for that mode (GLEC Framework defaults, well-to-wake). Set "Emissions priority" in the route planner to let carbon steer the recommended route.

      About

      Eastbound 3PL Index helps retailers that import into the US East Coast choose a route, a shipping mode and a 3PL. It's an early prototype. Transit times are typical estimates, freight rates come from dated public indexes, and tariff figures are estimates to confirm with a customs broker.

      How to use it

      1. Route planner: choose a factory region, 3PL, final stop and ship month. Switch news and weather scenarios on or off. The best route and two alternatives are highlighted on the graph and the map. The crowd-effect panel shows how other shippers' choices change your best route.
      2. Tariffs & duties: pick a product and shipment value to compare duties, fees, freight and landed cost for China, Vietnam and India.
      3. Mode advisor: choose a sector, weight, value and deadline to see whether air, a shared container or a full container is cheapest. It uses the route planner's scenario.
      4. When to book: pick a deadline, its date, and your cargo value to see which sailing is cheapest once other importers rush for the same one. It uses the route planner's scenario and the Mode advisor's 40' rate.
      5. 3PL index: filter providers by type or port. Use "Route it" to send one to the route planner.
      6. Ports: 2025 volumes for East Coast ports.
